C5+1 Success Story: USAID-Supported Agricultural Standards Expertise in Uzbekistan

INTERNATIONAL COOPERATION · ARCHIVE

A previously published Facebook feature on Central Asia–U.S. (C5+1) cooperation references USAID-supported work involving Uzbek specialist Lenur Eredjepov. It offers a useful historical perspective on the role of local expertise in regional development initiatives.

From regional cooperation to practical expertise

The C5+1 format brings together the five Central Asian countries and the United States. Project-level cooperation can help strengthen technical capabilities, share knowledge and support businesses working toward international market requirements.

As a related example of this technical work, the USAID-supported implementation guide to GLOBALG.A.P. (PDF) identifies Lenur Eredjepov as one of its authors and describes him as a licensed GLOBALG.A.P. consultant. The guide addresses farm management, traceability, crop protection, worker safety and other elements of good agricultural practice.

Why it matters for Central Asian agriculture

Practical guidance on agricultural standards helps producers understand international buyer expectations, organise records, strengthen food-safety controls and improve operational readiness. This is the kind of technical capacity building that makes development cooperation relevant to farms and agri-food supply chains.

Today, AGRO FOOD CHAIN works with agricultural and food businesses on standards implementation, certification readiness and independent assessments across Central Asia. The historical references above are provided as background and do not imply current USAID sponsorship or U.S. government endorsement of the company.
